Implications — what all of this actually means

The other pages report what was measured. This one asks the harder question: given those numbers, what follows — for the science, for anyone deploying a moderation pipeline, for the benchmark, and for the person carrying this to Lausanne. Every claim here is downstream of a number with a file behind it.

The one sentence, if you read nothing else
A deepfake moderation pipeline that trusts model confidence has a failure mode that no accuracy metric can see: an attacker who moves confidence without moving the label turns the safety mechanism — abstention — into the attack surface. We measured it, priced it, priced the fix, and found the fix costs moderator hours rather than accuracy.

1 · Scientific implications

Detection and trust are two different problems, and the field routinely conflates them

The sharpest result on the attacks page is that two attacks break the system in orthogonal ways. A realistic corruption (JPEG q30, 0.5× downscale) destroys accuracy — 0.8311 → 0.68 — while confidence ranking survives. The confidence attack (ACE) leaves accuracy bit-identical and destroys ranking — failure-AUROC 0.7703 → 0.0002.

The implication is methodological and it is not cosmetic: a single "robustness" number is meaningless, because a model can be robust on one axis and catastrophically fragile on the other. Any evaluation that does not print accuracy beside a ranking metric (AURC, failure-AUROC) cannot distinguish these, and the whole point of the moderation setting is that the second failure is the dangerous one. This is why src/metrics.py refuses to score one without the other.

Calibration is provably not a defence for an abstention rule — and that lands on the PIs' turf

For a binary model, maximum-softmax confidence is σ(|margin|/T), monotone in the logit margin, so temperature scaling cannot reorder samples. On the linear probes this is exact: AURC is bit-identical at T ∈ {0.5, 1, 2, 5, 10}. It is a proof, asserted as a test, not a citation. (On the trained checkpoints the representation saturates and the guarantee is about ordering only — the correction dissected on the epistemics page.)

Why this is the strategically important result
The PIs' own research area is evidential / Dirichlet uncertainty. This project proves that the cheap, standard fix — calibrate the softmax — cannot help a confidence-based abstention rule. That is exactly the gap a genuine uncertainty estimate is meant to fill. Arriving with the proof that the naïve baseline fails is arriving with the motivation for their method, quantified — a far stronger position than arriving with a half-built copy of their method.

Accuracy is defence-in-depth for selective moderation — the most useful non-obvious finding

The confidence attack collapses ranking on both detectors. On CLIP ViT-L/14, failure-AUROC falls 0.8779 → 0.1812 under the realisable attack — a relatively worse collapse than EfficientNet's. Yet through the moderation layer the outcomes diverge sharply:

under realisable ACE (1 grey level)EfficientNet-B0CLIP ViT-L/14
clean residual risk (5% SLA)4.65%5.46%
residual risk under attack33.06%7.46%
clean error rate (the ceiling)16.9%~6.8%

Residual risk is bounded by the underlying error rate: you cannot ship more wrong auto-decisions than the model gets wrong. CLIP has so few errors to mis-rank that even with its confidence fully attacked and 91% of traffic auto-decided, the damage is capped near 7%. The implication is genuinely useful and rarely stated: a better base detector is itself a confidence defence, even when its confidence signal is worthless under attack. Investing in raw accuracy and investing in confidence robustness are not separate budgets — the first partially buys the second. Source: runs/moderation_clip.md, runs/moderation_threatmodel.md.

Robustness budgets do not transfer from object recognition to forensics

Adversarial training at the standard ImageNet budget (8/255) collapses this task to constant output — both clean and robust accuracy pinned at the majority-class rate. At the forensic budget 2/255 the same code trains fine. The reason (detailed on defenses) is that deepfake evidence is small-amplitude, high-frequency residue, and an 8/255 ball is wide enough to erase it. The implication for the field: robustness results on forensic detectors reported at object-recognition epsilons may be measuring collapse rather than defence, and forensic robustness must be reported at forensic epsilons with the budget stated on every row.

2 · Deployment implications — for anyone running a moderation pipeline

Your accuracy dashboard is blind to the attack that matters
Under the confidence attack, accuracy is 0.8311 before and after, to four decimals. Every accuracy-based monitor, canary and regression test reads green while the residual risk of the auto-decisions goes 4.65% → 33.06%. Monitoring must watch a ranking quantity — failure-AUROC, or residual-risk-at-fixed-coverage — not accuracy. This is the single most important operational takeaway.

"Escalate on uncertainty" fails against an adversary who removes uncertainty

The intuitive moderation design — auto-decide the confident cases, send the uncertain ones to a human — assumes the model is honestly uncertain when it is wrong. The label-free over-confidence attack breaks exactly that assumption: it needs no ground-truth labels (so a real attacker can run it) and it drives coverage up, to 100% on the undefended model. It does not trigger human review — it suppresses it, by making the model confidently wrong. An abstention policy is only as trustworthy as the confidence signal it reads, and that signal is attackable.

Robustness is bought in moderator-hours, and that is a budget line

Adversarial training restores the moderation SLA — residual risk under a label attack goes 100% → 13% — but the price is the review rate roughly doubling, 27% → 50%. That is not an accuracy cost; it is a staffing cost. Twice as many images land in the human queue. The honest way to present a robustness gain in this setting is with its review-rate price in the same sentence, because a moderation team pays it in headcount. Source: runs/d4_*.json.

The SLA is a choice, not a fact

Every number here is anchored to a 5% residual-risk target chosen for the demonstration. A real platform sets it from the cost of a wrong auto-decision against the size of its review team. Change the SLA and every coverage, review-rate and threshold moves. The pipeline exposes it as a parameter and stamps it into the artifact for exactly this reason — see moderation.

3 · Benchmark implications — what a SID-Set number is worth

A one-line rule with no model — width == height → fake — scores 0.9785 on the test split, above every trained detector measured here. Label 1 is 100% square 1024×1024 PNG, label 2 100% square, only 4.3% of reals square. The implication is uncomfortable: a reported SID-Set accuracy near 0.98 is not evidence of forensic capability until it is read against 0.9785, not against chance — and a leaderboard that reads it against 0.5 is ranking models on how well they absorbed a metadata artifact. Benchmark hygiene here means publishing the no-model floor beside every model number, which src/baselines.py now does.

And the control could not fully clear our own model
Cropping every image square (so aspect ratio carries no label information) left CLIP's accuracy unchanged — 0.9289 → 0.9291 — which proves the model cannot read aspect ratio. It does not prove the model is not riding the laundered version of the shortcut: the cache re-encodes to JPEG q95, turning PNG-vs-JPEG provenance into a compression trace, and a frozen threshold on the decode scale factor still scores 0.9798. Whether the probe rides that residue is an open question with a named settling experiment (one identical JPEG pass over every image). The broader implication: forensic benchmarks need format- and compression-matched evaluation subsets, or the whole field risks measuring provenance and calling it forensics. See the dataset page.

4 · Strategic implications — for the camp and the person holding WP1+WP4

Whoever owns the measurement owns the conversation

The npz contract (uid / y / logits / condition) lets an attacked or robustly-trained model be scored without touching its code. Published on Day 1, it makes this harness the point every subgroup's model flows through — WP2's attacks, WP3's checkpoints, the uncertainty heads. That is not a tooling convenience; it is positional. The person who defines how the camp measures robustness sets the terms of every later comparison. Published on Day 4, everyone has already invented their own format and the leverage is gone.

The honest-limitations posture is the credibility, not a weakness

An adversarial audit of this project's own write-up found roughly ten overclaims — including a theoretical bound that was false on the trained checkpoints — and each was corrected against the artifacts (epistemics). Walking into a room of robustness researchers with the corrections visible — "here is what we claimed, here is the number that broke it, here is the fixed claim" — is precisely the behaviour that room respects. The two live open questions (does the model ride the compression shortcut; does adversarial training really protect confidence) are framed as questions for the PIs, not swept up. In a trustworthy-AI camp, demonstrated epistemic hygiene is the deliverable behind the deliverables.

The uncertainty (σ) seam is the highest-leverage unbuilt thing

The PIs' decision diagram routes on a per-image uncertainty score σ produced by methods other people build (MC-Dropout, deep ensembles, evidential heads). The current contract carries logits only — there is no σ slot. Extending it, and publishing the extension on Day 1, is what turns "a harness that scores my two detectors" into "the harness every uncertainty method in the camp is evaluated in." It is spelled out on the pathways page; it is the one contract change worth making before anyone writes a training loop.

5 · What is not implied — the guardrails

Equally important: the claims this project cannot yet make, so none of them are made by accident.

Not "AT protects confidence."The encouraging reading of the defense results rests on a single, under-optimised attack (effective ε only ~64% of budget on PGD-AT — a possible gradient-masking signature), and the under-confidence arm was never run. This is a question for the PIs, not a refutation of the Ledda hypothesis. Not "our detector is clean of the shortcut."The geometry control rules out aspect ratio, not the laundered compression trace. Open until the format-matched subset is run. Not "SID-Set test results."Every number is the SID-Set validation slice; the official test split is withheld by the dataset authors. Never present these as test-set numbers. Not the 98% figure as the deployable threat.The float32 ACE result (98% residual risk) needs post-decode tensor access. The 33.06% figure — one grey level, survivable in a saved file — is the one an attacker can actually produce, and the one to lead with.
