The project

What TrustFake is, the camp it belongs to, which pieces you own, and how the deck's one-line thesis turned into measured numbers.

TrustFake in one paragraph
TrustFake is a two-week research build on trustworthy AI for social-media image deepfake detection. The task is deliberately narrow: binary real-vs-fake classification over the SID-Set dataset (real / fully-synthetic / tampered images, folded to real vs fake). The frame is not "can we detect fakes" but "can a detector stay trustworthy inside a content moderation pipeline" — a pipeline that does not just read the predicted label, it reads the model's confidence to decide whether an image is auto-allowed, auto-flagged, or escalated to a human. The deck's own words: an attacker who can inflate a model's confidence can make a malicious image "bypass scrutiny and be automatically accepted," turning "a risk-aware system into an over-trusting one." That sentence is the whole project.

The camp

TrustFake is Project 02 at TReC 2026 — a two-week team-building research camp run under the TRAIL programme. You are not building this alone or over months; you are building it against a clock, in a subgroup, in front of two PIs whose own research is adversarial robustness and trustworthy vision.

The proposal splits the work into four scientific challenges (C1–C4), each mapped to one objective, one work package (WP1–WP4), and one deliverable (D1–D4). Four subgroups run these in parallel once the WP1 interfaces exist; the last day is reserved for integration and the talk, not new code.

What you hold, and what you deliberately do not build

You own the two ends of the pipeline: WP1 (the evaluation harness) and WP4 (the moderation layer). The two subgroups in the middle produce models — WP2 attacks them, WP3 hardens them. Your code never touches theirs; it consumes arrays. That separation is the point, and it is worth being explicit about one piece you are careful not to build.

The uncertainty heads are the PIs' own research — score them, do not build them
The proposal's background section leans on the PIs' home turf: uncertainty estimation methods "beyond standard softmax scores" — Monte-Carlo Dropout and deep ensembles (refs [13,14] in the deck). Those heads are Sournac and Ben Jmaa's research area. If your subgroup tried to build a better uncertainty head, you would be competing on the PIs' ground, entangled in their publication, and staking your two weeks on a modelling result you cannot guarantee. Instead the harness takes only (scores, labels) — it is agnostic to where the confidence came from (a plain max-softmax probability, a dropout ensemble, a trained head). So you can score any head anyone produces, including the PIs' own, without owning the risk of building one. The referee is not a player. Owning the scorer means every other subgroup's work — and the PIs' — is measured through your instrument, and your deliverable cannot be scooped by a modelling choice that does not land in time. See the harness page for the npz contract that makes this a data adapter, not a rebuild.

The deck's thesis, and how each clause now has a number

Figure 1 of the proposal carries the whole project as one line: "From data to trustworthy decisions: detect, stress-test, harden, and act." Four verbs, one per work package. Before the camp those were promises. After the pre-camp build (19 Aug, re-audited 19–23 Aug) each has a measured number attached. The numbers themselves live on their own pages — here is only the shape, so you can see that every clause turned into evidence rather than a slogan.

The one sentence to keep
Read down the "Act" row: a one-grey-level perturbation, invisible to a human and survivable in a saved PNG, takes a policy tuned to a 5% error budget to a third of its auto-decisions being wrong — with accuracy unchanged to four decimals. That is the thesis proven in a single row, and it is why the project measures confidence, not just correctness. The full walk-through is on the moderation page.

The TRAIL Factory brick

The proposal's Section 8 asks whether TrustFake yields a "reusable TRAIL Factory brick" — a component that outlives the two-week camp and can be dropped into later applied-AI work. The deck answers yes, and names the candidate: "a reusable evaluation and decision-support brick for trustworthy multimedia AI, helping organizations test whether visual AI systems remain reliable under realistic perturbations and adversarial manipulation."

That brick is WP1 + WP4 — the two pieces you hold. The harness is written to be exactly that: it scores (scores, labels) and nothing else, so — in the README's own phrasing — "a second dataset is a data adapter, not a rebuild." Swap deepfakes for any binary visual-trust decision and the same scorer, the same risk-coverage machinery, the same moderation policy apply unchanged. The camp is the first customer of a general instrument, not a one-off deliverable. Whether that framing survives contact with the PIs, and what a demonstrable brick needs by 4 Sep, is the pathways page.

Multidisciplinarity, stated plainly
TReC wants STEM crossed with social-sciences-and-humanities. TrustFake qualifies on its face: computer vision, adversarial ML and uncertainty on the STEM side; media reliability, misinformation, and the governance of AI-assisted moderation on the SSH side. The harness is the bridge — it turns a governance question ("should this be auto-decided?") into a measurable quantity ("residual risk at this coverage").
